# Occasions calendar and visual guide

Every Islamic, UAE national, corporate, CSR and international occasion I design for: when it falls – including how to plan for the ones that move each year – which design mode it takes, its motifs and palette, the imagery that would be culturally wrong, how the Arabic and English copy sit, and the camera moves and music that have worked. It is written for an audience in the UAE and the wider Gulf.

- **The look behind it:** [Style DNA](style-dna.md) (modes, palettes, guardrails) · [AI video and image generation](ai-video-and-image-generation.md) (models and prompt craft) · [AI music prompting](ai-music-prompting.md) (the Arabic element rule and ready prompts)
- **Tools:** [`sa_capcut_captions.py`](../tools/sa_capcut_captions.py) brings the subtitle files into CapCut as editable text · [`sa_translate.py`](../tools/sa_translate.py) drafts an Arabic subtitle file from English, keeping every timing, for review · [`sa_font.py`](../tools/sa_font.py) installs Arabic typefaces as real font files · [`sa_brand_stamp.py`](../tools/sa_brand_stamp.py) puts the genuine logo on a generated plate at pixel level · [`sa_kenburns.py`](../tools/sa_kenburns.py) gives real and archival photographs slow 9:16 motion without generating a pixel · [`sa_qa.py`](../tools/sa_qa.py) runs the pre-publish checks

## 1. How the dates work

**Two calendars.** Gregorian occasions fall on the same date every year. Islamic occasions follow the Hijri calendar, which is lunar: twelve months of 29 or 30 days, about 354–355 days a year. So each Islamic occasion arrives **about 10–11 days earlier** in the Gregorian calendar every year, and moves through every season in roughly 33 years. Never copy last year’s date.

**An Islamic day begins at sunset**, so an occasion starts on the evening before its Gregorian date.

**Ramadan and the two Eids depend on the moon.** The start of a month is confirmed by sighting the new crescent, and the official UAE announcement can come as late as the evening before. Astronomical calendars give the likely date, but it can land a day either side.

**How I plan for a moving date:**

1. Work from the expected date, and plan for it landing **one day either side**.
2. **Keep the date off the artwork.** A greeting doesn’t need one, and an artwork without a date survives a one-day shift.
3. Have the post finished and approved before the expected eve; publish once the official announcement is made.
4. Holiday lengths are announced each year. Check the announcement rather than assuming last year’s pattern.

**Two intervals that never change**, because they sit inside the Hijri calendar, are handy for planning a season:

- **Eid Al Adha falls 67–69 days after Eid Al Fitr** – roughly ten weeks.
- **Ramadan begins 32–34 days after Isra and Mi’raj** – roughly a month.

## 2. The calendar

### Islamic occasions (moving dates)

In Hijri order through the year:

| Occasion | Hijri date | Timing rule | Design mode | Palette |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mawlid Al Nabawi** | 12 Rabi’ al-Awwal (month 3) | Moves ~11 days earlier each year | Calligraphy-forward | Green and white |
| **Isra and Mi’raj** | 27 Rajab (month 7) | Moves ~11 days earlier each year | Calligraphy-forward | Deep green, white and gold |
| **Ramadan** | 1 Ramadan (month 9), for 29 or 30 days | Starts on the crescent sighting, often announced the evening before | Calligraphy-forward | Gold and amber calligraphy on a soft blue-grey |
| **Eid Al Fitr** | 1 Shawwal (month 10) | The day after Ramadan ends; confirmed by the crescent sighting | Calligraphy-forward, sacred | Deep green with gold and amber |
| **Eid Al Adha** | 10 Dhu al-Hijjah (month 12) | The day after the Day of Arafah (9 Dhu al-Hijjah); 67–69 days after Eid Al Fitr | Calligraphy-forward, sacred | Deep forest green with warm amber and gold |

### UAE national occasions (fixed dates)

| Occasion | Date | Timing rule | Design mode | Palette |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Emirati Women’s Day** | **28 August** | Fixed | Real photography | Warm neutrals with flag accents |
| **UAE Flag Day** | **3 November** | Fixed. Flags are raised together across the country at 11:00. Not a public holiday | Real photography or heritage | The flag’s colours |
| **Eid Al Etihad (UAE National Day)** | **2 December** | Fixed. Eid Al Etihad has been the official name for National Day since the 2023 celebrations – one occasion, not two. My older notes listed them separately; they are the same day | Heritage / archival | Deep green, red and white (the flag’s colours) |

**Not in my visual guide, but plan around it:** **Commemoration Day, 30 November**, two days before National Day, is a day of remembrance. Nothing celebratory goes out on it.

### Corporate occasions (event-driven)

| Occasion | When | Design mode | Palette |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Company milestones** – contracts, anniversaries, awards | When it happens; anniversaries on the founding date | 3D CGI / tech | Silver and neon green |
| **Safety milestones** – lost-time-injury-free man-hours | When the milestone is reached. **World Day for Safety and Health at Work, 28 April**, is a fixed hook | 3D CGI / tech, or real photography | Green, silver and white |

### CSR and awareness days

| Occasion | Date | Design mode | Palette |
|---|---|---|---|
| **World Wildlife Day** | **3 March** | Editorial stock | A bold natural palette |
| **Earth Day** | **22 April** | Editorial / nature, plus 3D CGI | Sage green and earth tones |
| **World Blood Donor Day**, and blood drives and health days | **14 June** for the world day; drives whenever they run | Real photography | Warm neutrals with a red accent |
| **Breast Cancer Awareness Month** | **October**, the whole month – charity runs and walks | Real photography | Pink and white |

### International occasions

| Occasion | Date | Design mode | Palette |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New Year** | **1 January** | 3D CGI / tech, or editorial | Gold on a dark background |
| **Mother’s Day** | **21 March** in the UAE and much of the Arab world – not the UK’s Mothering Sunday or the May date used in the US | Real photography | Warm, soft neutrals and pastels |

### The year at a glance

| Month | Fixed occasions |
|---|---|
| January | New Year (1) |
| March | World Wildlife Day (3) · Mother’s Day (21) |
| April | Earth Day (22) · World Day for Safety and Health at Work (28) |
| June | World Blood Donor Day (14) |
| August | Emirati Women’s Day (28) |

The five Islamic occasions float on top of this: any of them can fall in any month, about 11 days earlier each year. Work out each year’s dates from the Hijri date and the planning rules in section 1.

## 3. The five design modes

Pick the mode for the occasion and the background, palette and kind of imagery follow from it. More detail in [Style DNA, section 3](style-dna.md#3-background-style-by-mode-and-occasion).

| Mode | Looks like | Imagery comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Calligraphy-forward** | Large Arabic calligraphy as the hero, a white or light background, a faint mosque silhouette | Generated backgrounds with no text; the calligraphy is typeset separately |
| **3D CGI / tech** | Glossy 3D, a city skyline, cinematic light, silver and neon green | Generated scenes; the real logo composited in, never generated |
| **Real photography** | Real people, warm neutrals, a light semi-transparent brand-colour overlay | Real photographs only – never generated people |
| **Heritage / archival** | Black-and-white vintage photography, illustrated heritage icons (falcon, camel, dhow), green, red and white | Archival photographs and illustration |
| **Editorial stock** | Bold creative stock, large white text, minimal branding (the logo only) | Licensed stock |

## 4. Imagery that is culturally wrong

None of these is a craft error, and each would pass any technical check. That is why they are written down.

| Don’t | Do instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Show the Ka’bah or Masjid al-Haram for Eid Al Adha** – or Hajj imagery generally | A crescent moon, a desert night sky, a generic mosque silhouette, arabesque patterns, ornate arches | A standing rule, corrected after my own occasions notes once listed the Ka’bah as the “defining visual”. A generated holy site is never acceptable |
| **Use lanterns as the visual for Eid Al Adha** | Keep lanterns for **Ramadan and Eid Al Fitr**, where they are the signature | Lanterns read as Ramadan to this audience. A lanterns video made for Eid Al Adha was reassigned to Ramadan and Eid Al Fitr |
| **Describe a vehicle by its silhouette or light signature** | Name the exact make and model, show the **rear only**, and forbid logos, emblems, badges, model names, text and number plates in so many words | Light-signature descriptions drift towards the most famous matching brands, which a reviewer flagged at once as brand-unsafe |
| **Generate hands** | Real photography where hands matter (Earth Day, blood donation); otherwise frame them out | Generated hands are the most common giveaway. My occasion notes list “hands” as a motif for some CSR days: that means photographed hands, never generated ones |
| **Generate people, text or logos into a video** | Real people from real photography; text and the genuine logo added in the edit | Models garble lettering and invent plausible-looking brand text |
| **Show another city’s skyline** | The skyline of the city you are speaking to | Local viewers recognise their own skyline – and someone else’s – instantly |
| **Set long Arabic in a dense ornamental script** | Clean handwritten Naskh or Ruq’ah, generous leading, strict text-safe margins, no artwork behind the words | Diwani or Thuluth at full message length collided letterform against line; keep the ornate scripts for a short display line, if at all |
| **Shorten approved copy to fit a design** | Vary the treatment; every option carries the complete wording | Approved words belong to the person who approved them |
| **Generate the flag without checking it** | Check it against the real one: a vertical red band at the hoist, then horizontal green, white and black | A wrong band order is the first thing a national audience sees |

## 5. Arabic-first copy

- **On the artwork, Arabic leads** – first, largest, at the top – and English follows. The written caption under the post carries both languages in full, never a summary of one.
- **The logo is always bilingual and always in the same corner** (top right).
- **Greetings** (transliterated here; set them in Arabic on the artwork): *Ramadan Kareem* · *Eid Al Fitr Mubarak* · *Eid Al Adha Mubarak* · *Eid Mubarak* for either Eid. Pick one English spelling of each occasion name and hold it everywhere.
- **Never let an image model write the Arabic.** Generate the background with no text, then typeset the approved Arabic separately in a properly rendered Arabic font and check every line by eye. Models do not reproduce long Arabic exactly. [`sa_font.py`](../tools/sa_font.py) installs Google Fonts families as real files, so a render pipeline can use them.
- **Machine translation is a draft.** [`sa_translate.py`](../tools/sa_translate.py) turns an English subtitle file into Arabic locally, keeping every timing, for a fluent reviewer to correct – never for direct publication.

### Text timing in an 8-second occasion video

| Time | On screen |
|---|---|
| 0–2 s | The picture only – let it breathe |
| 2 s | The logo fades in, top right (placed by hand in CapCut) |
| 2–4.5 s | The Arabic headline alone |
| 4.5–6 s | Arabic headline and English headline |
| 6–8 s | Arabic, English and a short bilingual tagline, all three on screen |

**Four subtitle files ship with every selected video**, so the text stays editable in CapCut:

| File | Holds | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Arabic headline | The Arabic headline only | 2 s to 8 s |
| English headline | The English headline only | 4.5 s to 8 s |
| Tagline | The bilingual tagline | 6 s to 8 s |
| All combined | All three building in sequence | One track, for a single import |

## 6. Occasion by occasion

The default motion for every generated occasion video comes from [Style DNA](style-dna.md#1-video-style): slow, smooth and cinematic, one continuous eight-second shot with internal movement, god rays, dust, bokeh and depth of field; 9:16; three or four variations, from which I pick the hero; every prompt ending “no people, no text, no logos”. The camera moves below are only those from my own selected or tested occasion prompts.

### Eid Al Adha

- **Show:** a desert night sky, a crescent moon, a generic mosque silhouette, arabesque patterns, ornate pointed arches, god rays; deep green with warm amber and gold.
- **Avoid:** the Ka’bah, Masjid al-Haram and Hajj imagery; lanterns as the main visual.
- **Copy:** Arabic leads (*Eid Al Adha Mubarak*), English follows, then a short bilingual tagline.
- **Camera move – approved:** a slow cinematic push forward towards an ornate arch.

  > Slow cinematic push forward toward an ornate Islamic pointed arch filled with intricate cream arabesque carved geometric patterns, soft mint green background, golden crescent moon resting at the base, white hanging lantern softly glowing upper left, warm cream and gold tones, gentle god rays filtering through the arch, floating dust particles, sacred and elegant 3D render atmosphere, ultra-cinematic, film grain, 8K, no people, no text, no logos

  The version with space kept clear for text on the right:

  > Slow cinematic push forward toward an ornate Islamic pointed arch on the LEFT side of frame, intricate cream arabesque carved geometric patterns filling the arch, soft mint teal green background, golden crescent moon resting at the base of the arch, gold hanging lantern softly glowing upper left corner, open empty negative space on the RIGHT two-thirds of frame, warm cream and gold tones, gentle god rays filtering through, floating dust particles, sacred and elegant 3D render atmosphere, ultra-cinematic, film grain, 8K, no people, no text, no logos

  Why it worked: the arch, arabesque, gold crescent, soft mint and god rays give a premium 3D-render feel without any holy-site imagery. It ran on Kling 3.0 when my primary video tool’s credits ran out. **One correction since:** it was approved with a small lantern as an accent, before I made lanterns a Ramadan-and-Eid-Al-Fitr motif. Reusing it for Eid Al Adha today, I drop the lantern clause.

- **Generated but not selected** – kept as alternatives, not winners:

  > Slow cinematic wide shot of a vast desert at twilight, a luminous crescent moon rising above soft sand dunes, deep teal and midnight blue sky with warm amber horizon glow, a single glowing lantern in the foreground, ethereal mist over the dunes, sacred and peaceful atmosphere, slow camera push forward, ultra-cinematic, film grain, no people, no text, no logos

  > Slow cinematic tilt upward through an ornate mosque interior, intricate geometric tilework and carved arches in deep forest green and gold, shafts of god rays streaming through high windows, floating dust particles, deep shadows and warm amber pools of light, sacred reverent atmosphere, smooth fluid camera movement, ultra-cinematic, 8K, film grain, no people, no text, no logos

  (The first carries a lantern too; drop it for Eid Al Adha.) A third variation – an aerial pull-back revealing a holy site at golden hour – is the one the no-holy-sites rule now forbids.

### Ramadan and Eid Al Fitr

- **Show:** lanterns (Ramadan’s signature), a crescent moon and star, a mosque silhouette, an iftar table, dates, warm glowing light; for Eid Al Fitr, lanterns, a crescent, a generic mosque, light rays and arabesque.
- **Palette:** gold and amber calligraphy on a soft blue-grey for Ramadan; deep green with gold and amber for Eid Al Fitr.
- **Avoid:** holy sites.
- **Copy:** *Ramadan Kareem*; *Eid Al Fitr Mubarak*.
- **Camera move – selected (from four variations):** a slow cinematic upward drift through lanterns.

  > Slow cinematic upward drift through a softly lit interior, clusters of ornate Islamic lanterns hanging in warm amber and gold light, deep forest green shadows with glowing emerald reflections, rich bokeh in background, gentle swaying motion of lanterns, god rays filtering through, sacred and reverent atmosphere, ultra-cinematic, film grain, 8K detail, no people, no text, no logos

  Why it worked: warm gold against deep green, a sacred atmosphere, god rays and gentle motion. It was generated for Eid Al Adha and reassigned here when the lantern rule was set.

### Mawlid Al Nabawi and Isra and Mi’raj

- **Mawlid – show:** a mosque, Arabic calligraphy, green sacred geometry; green and white.
- **Isra and Mi’raj – show:** a night sky, a mosque, Arabic calligraphy, celestial elements; deep green, white and gold.
- **Both:** calligraphy-forward, so the calligraphy is the hero and is typeset, never generated. No approved generated prompt yet; the arch and lantern grammar above (slow push or slow upward drift, god rays, sacred atmosphere) is my starting point.

### Eid Al Etihad (National Day)

- **Mode:** heritage and archival. Black-and-white vintage photography, illustrated heritage icons (falcon, camel, dhow), the desert, a fort, the flag, the skyline of the city you are addressing.
- **Palette:** deep green, red and white.
- **Copy:** Arabic leads, bilingual throughout.
- **Camera move:** no approved generated prompt yet. Archival photographs get slow, real-pixel push-ins with [`sa_kenburns.py`](../tools/sa_kenburns.py) rather than being regenerated.
- **Watch the dates around it:** Flag Day on 3 November, Commemoration Day on 30 November.

### UAE Flag Day

- **Show:** the flag being raised, team unity, national pride – real photographs of a real flag-raising, ideally your own at 11:00 on the day.
- **Palette:** the flag’s colours. Check the flag itself (section 4).

### Emirati Women’s Day

- **Mode:** real photography. Emirati women in traditional dress, empowerment, community – real people, photographed, never generated.
- **Palette:** warm neutrals with flag accents.
- **Personal note cards** – a treatment I made for individual gifts: three portrait, handwritten-style Arabic stationery options, each carrying the complete approved message addressed to the recipient: ivory with an embossed palm motif in the brand green; deep green with antique-gold florals; warm sand with a sage watercolour. The backgrounds were generated with no text and the exact Arabic typeset afterwards. A dense Diwani or Thuluth setting collided at that length, so the direction moved to a clean handwritten Naskh or Ruq’ah.

### Company and safety milestones

- **Company milestones:** 3D CGI / tech – the genuine logo in 3D (composited, never generated), a city skyline, abstract energy beams; silver and neon green; the achievement headline in both languages.
- **Safety milestones:** construction and engineering workers, safety helmets, the skyline; green, silver and white. Workers are real people, so real photography; generated scenes stay empty of people.
- **Camera move:** no approved generated prompt yet.

### CSR and awareness days

- **Earth Day:** nature, a globe, plants, clean energy, photographed hands; sage green and earth tones.
- **World Wildlife Day:** wildlife photography, nature and conservation, from licensed editorial stock; a bold natural palette.
- **Breast Cancer Awareness Month:** community, running, togetherness, hope; pink and white; real photography. A campaign that ran one dedicated hashtag across a whole series of posts kept its audience from post to post.
- **Blood donation and health days:** people, community care, photographed hands; warm neutrals with a red accent.

### International occasions

- **New Year:** fireworks, a skyline, a countdown; gold on a dark background.
- **Mother’s Day (21 March):** mother-and-child moments, warmth, emotion; warm soft neutrals and pastels; real photography.

## 7. Music by occasion

Labelled honestly: one lesson here was tested on an occasion track; the rest is direction from my standing music rules, not yet tested occasion by occasion. Prompts and the full rules are in [AI music prompting](ai-music-prompting.md).

**Always:** Arabic or Emirati identity a listener can actually hear – an oud or qanun with a real melodic role inside a cinematic, film-score arrangement. My first direction, “felt, not heard”, was judged to have no Arabic character at all.
**Never:** Arabic jazz, lounge, piano as the lead, Gulf folk or wedding styles, trance or EDM, saxophone, generic corporate muzak.

| Occasion family | Direction | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Islamic occasions** | Reverent and cinematic; the Arabic identity present but the percussion kept well back – nothing that sounds like a wedding | **Tested:** when a reviewer found the Arabic beat too prominent on an occasion track, the fix was to pitch the whole file down about two semitones (`asetrate` × 0.88) or three (× 0.84) in ffmpeg rather than regenerate – the tempo drops by the same ratio |
| **National occasions** | Heritage prestige: a solo oud opening, strings swelling through the middle and a final lift; a Hijaz-mode oud lead, deep frame percussion, low brass, a heroic wide-screen mix | From the brand-music prompt that got closest to approval; not yet used on a National Day piece |
| **Women’s Day, Mother’s Day, tributes** | One continuous emotional bed – piano or strings – running into the outro, with no jarring cuts in the score | **Tested** on delivered tribute montages ([Style DNA, 1b](style-dna.md#1b-tribute-and-farewell-montage-style)); not yet on a Women’s Day or Mother’s Day piece |
| **Milestones and CSR** | Cinematic orchestral, confident and hopeful | Direction only |

**For a live occasion event:**

- **Do the arithmetic before choosing tracks.** Three tracks do not fill an 85-minute room. Long-form beds of one to four hours let each slot run without a repeat or an audible loop.
- **Play ad-free, with autoplay off**, so the room never hears an advert or rolls into an unrelated video – and never put a streaming app’s interface on the event screen.
- **Shape long cues:** build, a first hit at about 1:30, a drop to near-silence at about 2:15, rebuild, the full climax at about 4:00, then ring out. Five minutes at constant maximum stops registering after about 90 seconds; the drop is what makes the return land.

## 8. Checklist for each occasion

1. **Confirm the date.** For an Islamic occasion, plan for one day either side and wait for the official announcement before publishing.
2. **Pick the mode** (section 3); the palette and the kind of imagery follow.
3. **Real or generated?** Anything with real people is photographed. Generated scenes carry no people, no text and no logos.
4. **Generate three or four variations** at 9:16, eight seconds, using the formula in [Style DNA](style-dna.md#1-video-style). I pick the hero.
5. **Check every frame against section 4**, at 200%.
6. **Typeset the Arabic and the English separately**, build the four subtitle files, add the genuine logo.
7. **Lay the music** (section 7).
8. **Run the pre-publish checks** with [`sa_qa.py`](../tools/sa_qa.py), and – for a moving date – publish only after the official announcement.

**Three things I learnt reviewing ten months of one brand’s posts:** key occasions get **video**, never a single image; **human emotion beats corporate announcement** every time; and a lone awareness-day image post barely registers, so either commit to a proper piece or a sustained campaign, or let the day pass.
