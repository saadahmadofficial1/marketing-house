# Seeing your workspace as a map (Obsidian)

Optional, and it takes two minutes. Everything your assistant knows about you is stored as
plain text files in this folder. Obsidian is a free app that displays those files as a
browsable, searchable, linked map — so you can look at your own knowledge instead of taking
its word for what it remembers.

**You do not need it.** The system works identically without it. Some people find seeing the
map is what makes it click.

---

## Setting it up

1. Download Obsidian (free, no account needed): **https://obsidian.md**
2. Open it → **Open folder as vault** → choose this folder.
3. That is it. Obsidian creates its own settings folder (`.obsidian/`) the first time you
   open the folder; the defaults are fine.

---

## What you get

- **Search everything you have ever told it**, instantly, including old projects.
- **The graph view** (the constellation icon on the left) — every note as a dot, every link as
  a line. Clusters are your real areas of work. Lonely dots are things nobody linked.
- **Backlinks** — open any note and see everything that refers to it.
- **Edit anything by hand.** Your assistant reads the same files, so a correction you type
  into a note is a correction it inherits.

## Worth knowing

- `memory/` is what it knows about you. `Projects/` is your live work. `Sessions/` is its
  place-keeping — you can ignore that one.
- Editing a file by hand is fine and encouraged. Deleting one makes it forget that thing.
- Do not install community plugins on a work machine unless someone has checked them —
  plugins can read every file in the vault.
- If you also set up the search brain (`brain/`), the two work together: Obsidian for
  browsing and editing by hand, the brain for asking questions in plain language.
